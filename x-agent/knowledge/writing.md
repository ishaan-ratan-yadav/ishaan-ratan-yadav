# Writing rules for X

## Hooks (first line decides everything)
Write 5–10 hooks per post, then pick. The first line must stop a scrolling artist. Hook types (tag them;
examples only, real posts use real facts):
- **time/effort result**: "This 6-second shot took 41 hours."
- **contrarian**: "Stop charging per second of animation."
- **curiosity gap**: "The client rejected this. Then they paid 3x for it."
- **number list**: "7 things that make a 3D ad look expensive:"
- **story open**: "My first client paid me in exposure. Here's how I got the second one to pay $4,000."
- **prediction**: "In 12 months, 'AI or 3D?' won't be a question clients ask."
- **challenge / question**: "Name a brand with worse ads than its product."
- **pattern interrupt**: a visual-first line ("Look at the reflection.")
- **specific number**: "$0 → $12k/month as a 3D freelancer, 18 months, no agency."

## Style
- Plain, specific, a little cocky, never corporate. Write like a working artist talking to other artists.
- Short lines. One idea per post. Concrete nouns and numbers beat adjectives.
- Real experience only. Never invent clients, numbers, or stories; ask Ishaan in the pack if a fact is needed
  (mark the post "NEEDS FACT CHECK").
- End with a reason to reply when it fits (question, choice, "what would you cut?"). Not on every post.
- English for the global audience. Hinglish only if the weekly review proves it works.

## Banned (sounds AI-written, hurts dwell)
Em dashes (—), "delve", "game-changer", "unleash", "elevate", "in today's fast-paced world", "here's the thing",
"let that sink in", "it's not X, it's Y" constructions, rule-of-three padding, "As a 3D artist,",
generic praise replies ("Great post!", "So true 🔥"), emoji walls, more than two hashtags, hashtags in replies.

## Replies
- Must add something: a fact, experience, counterpoint, joke, or visual. If you can't add anything, skip the post.
- Under 200 chars usually. Lead with the point.
- Never self-promote in a reply unless asked ("we do this, DM" is spam).
